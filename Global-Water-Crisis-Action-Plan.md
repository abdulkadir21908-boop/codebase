# The Global Water Crisis
## Scale of the Problem and a Practical Action Plan

**Research compiled:** October 2026
**Sources:** 100+ references from 40+ organisations across UN agencies, national governments, universities, peer-reviewed journals, NGOs and industry bodies.

---

# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

- **2.1 billion people** — roughly **1 in 4 of humanity** — still lack safely managed drinking water ([WHO/UNICEF JMP 2025](https://www.who.int/news/item/26-08-2025-1-in-4-people-globally-still-lack-access-to-safe-drinking-water---who--unicef))
- **4 billion people** — almost **two thirds of the world's population** — experience **severe water scarcity for at least one month every year** ([UNICEF](https://www.unicef.org/wash/water-scarcity))
- **106 million people** drink directly from untreated surface water ([JMP 2025](https://washdata.org/reports/jmp-2025-wash-households))
- **~1.4 million deaths a year** are attributable to unsafe water, sanitation and hygiene ([Lancet Planetary Health / PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10290941))
- Agriculture consumes **~70% of all freshwater withdrawals** ([UNESCO WWDR 2024](https://www.unesco.org/reports/wwdr/en/2024/s))
- Progress is **off track**: the world must accelerate drinking-water progress **6×**, sanitation **5×**, and hygiene **3×** to hit 2030 targets ([UN Goal 6](https://sdgs.un.org/goals/goal6))

The problem is solvable. The technology exists — solar pumping, rainwater harvesting, desalination, wastewater reuse, and efficient irrigation are all proven and cost-effective. What is missing is **speed, financing, and political will**.

This document is structured in two halves: **Part A** establishes what the evidence says, and **Part B** gives a tiered action plan — from what an individual can do tonight to what governments, investors and institutions must do at scale.

# PART A — THE EVIDENCE

## A1. How many people drink unsafe water?

### The "safely managed" standard
The WHO/UNICEF Joint Monitoring Programme (JMP) uses a **five-rung service ladder**. A service is only "safely managed" if the water is (1) from an improved source, (2) **available on the premises**, (3) **available when needed**, and (4) **free from faecal and priority chemical contamination** ([JMP](https://washdata.org/reports/jmp-2025-wash-households)).

| Service level | People (2024) | Share of world | What it means |
|---|---|---|---|
| Safely managed | ~5.7 billion | 74% | On premises, always available, uncontaminated |
| Basic (but not safely managed) | 1.4 billion | ~18% | Improved source within 30 min round trip |
| Limited | 287 million | 3.6% | Improved source >30 min away |
| Unimproved | 302 million | 3.8% | Unprotected wells, springs, dug-outs |
| Surface water | 106 million | 1.3% | Rivers, lakes, ponds, irrigation canals |
| **Total lacking safely managed** | **2.1 billion** | **26%** | |

*Sources: [UNICEF DATA](https://data.unicef.org/resources/jmp-report-2025), [UN News](https://news.un.org/en/story/2025/08/1165722), [Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/what-no-safe-water-means)*

### Regional breakdown

**Africa** — the continent is furthest behind ([UNICEF](https://www.unicef.org/senegal/en/press-releases/africa-drastically-accelerate-progress-water-sanitation-and-hygiene-report)):
- 418 million lack even basic drinking water
- 779 million lack basic sanitation (208 million practise open defecation)
- 839 million lack basic hygiene services
- **Rural:** 4 in 5 lack safely managed water, 3 in 4 lack safely managed sanitation
- **Urban:** 2 in 5 lack safely managed water, 2 in 3 lack safely managed sanitation

**India** — 17% of world population, 4% of world water resources ([SIWI](https://siwi.org/news/groundwater-crises-threaten-the-poor-in-india)):
- 342 million lack safe water; 539 million lack a safe toilet ([Water.org](https://water.org/our-impact/where-we-work/india))
- 820 million Indians face high-to-extreme water stress
- 200,000 deaths/year from inadequate water and sanitation
- 26% of India's 6,762 groundwater blocks are over-exploited, critical or semi-critical ([CGWB 2025 via ORF](https://www.orfonline.org/expert-speak/arresting-india-s-groundwater-depletion-to-avert-water-bankruptcy))
- Extracts ~247 billion cubic metres/year — rivaling the US and China combined
- Per capita water availability down 70% since 1950

**Global physical supply** ([WMO State of Global Water Resources 2025](https://wmo.int/resources/publication-series/state-of-global-water-resources/2025)):
- 2025 was one of the **driest years for river discharge in 35 years**
- The past 7 years have seen the **fewest "normal" river flows since 1991**
- Earth's land water storage has **declined for 10 consecutive years**
- Glaciers lost mass in all regions for the **4th consecutive year**
- Asia and Africa accounted for >50% of water-related extreme events and **>80% of associated deaths**

> Earth is 70% water, but only ~3% is fresh, and **less than 1% is accessible for human use** ([WWF](https://www.worldwildlife.org/resources/facts/water-scarcity-8-facts-you-need-to-know)).

## A2. Why it matters

### Health
- **1.4 million deaths** and **74 million DALYs** per year attributable to unsafe WASH — 2.5% of all global deaths, and **7.6% of deaths in children under 5** ([Lancet/PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10290941))
- **395,000 under-5 deaths per year** could be avoided by addressing unsafe water, sanitation and hand hygiene ([WHO fact sheet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/drinking-water))
- **~1 million deaths/year** from diarrhoea linked to unsafe drinking water, sanitation and hand hygiene
- A child dies of a water- or sanitation-related disease **every 2 minutes**
- Over **50 species of disease** are linked to poor drinking-water quality ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/environmental-science/articles/10.3389/fenvs.2022.880246/full))
- WHO runs a scheme testing household water treatment products against health-based performance criteria ([WHO](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/drinking-water))
- Cholera outbreaks cluster in countries with the **lowest safe water and sanitation coverage**

### Education
- **Up to 443 million school days lost every year** to water-related illness ([WaterAid](https://www.wateraid.org/us/water-crisis-facts-and-statistics))
- A shortage of water in schools reduces enrolment, attendance and performance, especially for girls

### Gender and safety
- Women and girls spend an estimated **250 million hours every day** collecting water ([UN WWDR 2026 / UNESCO](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/global-water-crisis-aggravated-gender-inequalities-according-new-un-report))
- Women collect water in **over 70% of unserved rural households**
- Girls under 15 (7%) are more likely than boys (4%) to fetch water
- Adult women and teenage girls spend **~4 hours/week** on water vs. 1 hour for adult men ([World Bank](https://datatopics.worldbank.org/sdgatlas/goal-5-gender-equality))
- In ~4 out of 5 households without piped water, women and girls do the collection ([UN-Water](https://www.worldbank.org/en/data/interactive/2024/03/13/gendered-burden-of-water-collection-in-afe-afw-sub-saharan-africa))
- Each year a girl stays in school raises her expected adult income by ~20% ([Water.org](https://water.org/our-impact/water-crisis/womens-crisis))
- Collection trips of 30–60+ minutes carry physical-strain, road-safety and exploitation risks

### Economy
- **$260 billion/year** lost to inadequate access to basic water and sanitation ([Boston College](https://ccc.bc.edu/content/bc-ccc/news/blogs/understanding-water-scarcity.html))
- Every **US$1** invested in improved sanitation returns **~US$5.5** in global economic benefit ([UN](https://www.un.org/en/global-issues/water))
- Water deficits were linked to a **10% increase in global migration** between 1970–2000 ([WWDR 2024](https://www.unesco.org/reports/wwdr/en/2024/s))

### Conflict and security
- Only **32 of 153** countries have >90% of transboundary water area covered by an operating agreement ([WWDR 2024](https://www.waterdiplomat.org/story/2024/03/2024-world-water-development-report-dedicated-water-prosperity-and-peace))
- Water inequality amplifies social tension and conflict; water cooperation can build peace

## A3. Root causes

| Cause | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Climate change** | Higher temperatures, more floods and droughts. Limiting warming to 1.5°C rather than 2°C would **roughly halve** the population exposed to water scarcity ([UN Climate](https://www.un.org/en/climatechange/science/climate-issues/water)) |
| **Agricultural dominance** | ~70% of freshwater withdrawals; ~2,000–5,000 litres of water per person per day to produce food |
| **Population & urbanisation** | Developing 9 billion people by 2050 needs ~50% more food production and ~15% more water withdrawals ([World Bank Data Blog](https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/opendata/chart-globally-70-freshwater-used-agriculture)) |
| **Groundwater over-extraction** | Aquifers abstracted far faster than recharge; >170 million hectares of irrigated cropland under high or very high water stress ([UNU](https://unu.edu/inweh/news/world-enters-era-of-global-water-bankruptcy)) |
| **Ecosystem loss** | ~410 million hectares of wetlands lost in 50 years — nearly the land area of the EU; >half of the world's large lakes have shrunk since the early 1990s |
| **Pollution** | ~80% of global wastewater is released without treatment ([Water Technologies](https://www.watertechnologies.com/sites/default/files/documents/Water%20Reuse%20Drives%20the%20Resource%20Revolution%20Infographic.pdf)) |
| **Poverty & inequality** | LDCs are over **2×** as likely to lack basic WASH and **3×** as likely to lack basic hygiene ([UNRIC](https://unric.org/en/billions-still-lack-access-to-safe-water-sanitation-and-hygiene-warns-who-and-unicef-report)) |
| **Financing failure** | Progress must accelerate **6×/5×/3×** for water/sanitation/hygiene. Current rate is ~1% annual; ~3% needed |

# PART B — THE ACTION PLAN

> **Principle:** The problem is not a shortage of technology. It is a shortage of **deployment**. The actions below are ordered by **sphere of influence** — start at Level 1, and escalate to the level you actually control.

---

## LEVEL 1 — INDIVIDUAL (start today, no cost or near-zero cost)

### 1.1 Drink less water, waste less water
US household use averages **~300 gallons (1,135 L) per day**, ~70% of it indoors ([EPA WaterSense](https://www.epa.gov/watersense/statistics-and-facts)).

| Action | Typical saving |
|---|---|
| Fix leaks — leaks alone waste **18 gallons/household/day** | up to 10,000 gal/yr |
| Install a WaterSense showerhead (2.0 gpm vs 2.5 gpm standard) | ~2,700 gal/yr per person |
| Turn off the tap while brushing teeth / soaping dishes | ~3,000 gal/yr |
| Wash full loads only; use eco cycle | ~3,000 gal/yr |
| Sweep instead of hosing driveways; water plants at dawn, deep and infrequent | thousands of gallons |
| Replace toilets with WaterSense models (1.28 gpf vs 1.6–3.5 gpf) | 13,000 gal/yr per home |

*Sources: [EPA WaterSense](https://www.epa.gov/watersense/statistics-and-facts), [Water Calculator](https://watercalculator.org/footprint/indoor-water-use-at-home), [Green Home Technology Center](https://greenhome.osu.edu/water), [Clean Water Center](https://www.cleanwatercenter.com/about-us/news-and-events/42789-how-much-water-does-an-average-household-use.html)*

### 1.2 Cut your food water footprint
Food is the **largest single component** of most people's water footprint — far bigger than household use ([Water Calculator](https://watercalculator.org/how-to-save-water/change-your-diet)).

- A "healthy meat" diet reduces water footprint by up to **35%**; a vegetarian or pescatarian diet by up to **55%** ([European Commission study via Water Calculator](https://watercalculator.org/news/news-briefs/meat-european-cut-water-footprint))
- A typical person's diet has a footprint of ~**2,378 gallons (9,000 L)/day** in the US
- Cutting meat and dairy is effective **in high-income countries**; in low-income countries, greater intake of meat, dairy and eggs is advisable for nutrition ([Johns Hopkins study, via Water Calculator](https://watercalculator.org/how-to-save-water/change-your-diet))
- **Eat lower on the food chain**, choose whole over processed foods, and buy only what you will eat
- **One-third of all food produced is wasted** — buying and eating what you need directly saves the embedded water ([GREEN.org](https://green.org/lifestyle/how-to-reduce-your-food-footprint-practical-steps-for-a-sustainable-diet))

### 1.3 Ditch single-use plastic
- **1.3 billion** plastic bottles used per day worldwide (~1 million per minute) ([Aquasana](https://www.aquasana.com/info/important-plastic-water-bottle-stats-pd.html), [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/graphics/ENVIRONMENT-PLASTIC/0100B275155/index.html))
- It takes **3× the volume of water** to manufacture one bottle of water as it does to fill it
- Bottled water contains up to **~240,000 micro/nanoplastics per litre** ([NIH](https://www.nih.gov/news-events/nih-research-matters/plastic-particles-bottled-water), [Earth.org](https://earth.org/one-liter-of-bottled-water-contains-about-240000-plastic-particles-new-research-finds))
- **Action:** carry a refillable bottle; install a countertop filter if tap water is unsafe where you live

### 1.4 Use your voice
- Contact your local utility and MP about water pricing, leakage, and infrastructure investment
- Ask where your wastewater goes; demand reuse rather than discharge
- Join a water stewardship programme at work or school
- Vote and advocate for water as a **human right**

## LEVEL 2 — HOUSEHOLD / COMMUNITY (rainwater, reuse, shared systems)

### 2.1 Harvest rainwater
Rainwater harvesting is one of the cheapest, most scalable interventions available ([Global Center on Adaptation](https://gca.org/harvesting-rainwater-a-sustainable-solution-to-the-water-challenges-we-face)).

- Where rural population exceeds 50%, implementing RWH could **increase safely managed drinking water coverage by over 25%** ([PNAS/PMC 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12722354))
- Educational institutions can replace **69.6–80.6%** of potable water demand with harvested rainwater ([MDPI review](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4441/14/17/2716))
- Optimal scenario: a **2.7 m³ tank can replace up to 33% of total household water use**; scenarios cut storage demand by 5.5–84.5% ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0301479724024526))
- A 3,000 sq ft roof with 30,000 gallons of storage can meet **indoor household water needs statewide**, even under drought conditions ([Texas State University, Meadows Center](https://news.txst.edu/research-and-innovation/2025/meadows-center-study-examines-rainwater-harvesting.html))
- Benefits: cuts pressure on mains supply, recharges groundwater, prevents seawater intrusion, reduces stormwater runoff
- **Must-have:** first-flush diverters, screens, first-class filters, and screened/covered tanks to prevent vector-borne disease

### 2.2 Reuse water on site
- **Greywater** (shower, laundry, handwash) can be reclaimed for irrigation and toilet flushing
- Install **rain/soil sensors and smart irrigation** — up to 50% of outdoor water use is lost to inefficient watering ([Ohio State](https://greenhome.osu.edu/water))
- Practise **xeriscaping** with native, drought-tolerant species
- Use **mulching** and **compost** to reduce irrigation demand

### 2.3 Build shared community systems
- Community-managed boreholes with **solar pumps** (see Level 3.2)
- Rainwater harvesting at school and clinic level — double dividend: water security + improved attendance
- Local water committees with **female leadership** — evidence from South Asia and East Africa shows this sustains maintenance and reduces conflict
- Emergency water storage for drought/flood preparedness

## LEVEL 3 — TECHNICAL / INFRASTRUCTURE SOLUTIONS

### 3.1 Wastewater reuse — the biggest untapped resource
Over **80% of wastewater globally is discharged without treatment** ([Water Technologies](https://www.watertechnologies.com/sites/default/files/documents/Water%20Reuse%20Drives%20the%20Resource%20Revolution%20Infographic.pdf)).

- The untapped reuse potential is **~320 billion m³/year** — enough to supply **more than 10× the current global desalination capacity** ([UN-Water](https://www.unwater.org/water-facts/water-quality-and-wastewater))
- Water use splits roughly: **household 12%, industry 59%, agriculture 29%** — household conservation alone is a small part of the solution
- Leading companies have cut water consumption by **up to 90%** through wastewater recycling
- Over **500 facilities in the US** already recycle water to meet community needs ([US EPA](https://www.epa.gov/waterreuse/basic-information-about-water-reuse))
- The **EU Water Reuse Regulation** provides a legal framework, but reuse is still deployed well below potential due to weak public awareness and incoherent frameworks ([European Commission](https://environment.ec.europa.eu/topics/water/water-reuse_en))
- **Action for cities:** separate sewer systems, advanced treatment (MBR, ozonation, UV, RO), groundwater recharge with highly treated effluent, potable reuse in new-builds

### 3.2 Solar pumping for rural supply
Solar pumping is now proven **viable and cost-effective** for rural water supply across eight countries reviewed by Water Mission ([Water Mission](https://watermission.org/sites/default/files/2025-01/Water%20Mission-Solar%20Pumping%20for%20Rural%20Water%20Supply.pdf)).

- Solar pumping life-cycle costs fall **within or below IRC WASHCost benchmarks** for both piped schemes and boreholes with handpumps
- For medium/intermediate schemes, solar is **at least as cost-effective as handpumps while delivering higher service levels** in quality, accessibility and reliability
- Solar is most competitive where solar insolation is high: Africa, South America, South Asia, Southeast Asia ([World Bank](https://www.worldbank.org/en/topic/water/brief/solar-pumping))
- Case study: Derequa, Mozambique — off-grid village where diesel generators failed on cost; a rehabilitated borehole with a submersible solar pump now serves **nine water points**
- Handpump benchmark costs: 15m pump ~$700, 25m ~$1,200, 40m ~$1,900; drilling $60–200/m; maintenance ~15%/yr
- **Action:** NGOs and governments should shift rural water procurement from diesel/grid to solar by default

### 3.3 Rainwater harvesting at scale
Covered in 2.1. At city scale, combine rooftop collection, storm-drain capture, and aquifer recharge.

### 3.4 Desalination — necessary but must improve
- **MENA holds almost half of global online desalination capacity** (5,000+ plants); expected expansion of **1.5–3.5 million m³/day per year** between 2026 and 2030 ([World Bank MENA paper](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099120325085522367/pdf/P510972-95cd0c55-9926-4bca-a6fe-422af95faaff.pdf))
- Top five by capacity: Saudi Arabia (17.3 MCM/d), China (14.0), USA (11.0), UAE (9.1), Spain (5.0)
- Costs range **$0.49–$2.86/m³**; large RO plants (>100 MLD) typically **$0.85–$1.10/m³**; small RO **$1.40–$1.50/m³**; MSF from $1.75/m³
- EPC prices typically **$2–6 million per MCM/year of capacity**
- Remote island communities have paid **$93/m³** for emergency shipped water — desalination makes obvious economic sense there
- **Problems:** brine discharge, marine ecosystem damage, high energy use, expensive to decarbonise
- **The innovation bet:** [XPRIZE Water Scarcity](https://www.xprize.org/competitions/water) — a **$119 million, 5-year** competition to build reliable, affordable, sustainable seawater desalination. Semifinals testing 2026 → finalists 2027 → finals 2027–28 → winners 2028. Sponsored by the Mohamed Bin Zayed Water Initiative.
- Also under way: [Water Abundance XPRIZE / HeroX](https://www.herox.com/water) — extracting water from the atmosphere using no fossil fuel
- **By 2030 the world is projected to face a 40% gap between water supply and demand** ([XPRIZE](https://www.xprize.org/news/water-scarcity-semifinalists-advance-to-next-stage-of-119m-competition))

### 3.5 Fog harvesting and atmospheric water
Established in arid coastal regions (Chile, Peru, Namibia, Morocco). Best used as a supplement, not a primary supply.

### 3.6 Wastewater as energy and nutrients
Untreated wastewater contains recoverable energy, nutrients and by-products — turning a pollution problem into a resource ([WWDR 2024](https://www.unesco.org/en/reports/wwdr/2024))

## LEVEL 4 — AGRICULTURE (the 70% lever)

Agriculture takes ~70% of freshwater withdrawals; industry ~20%; domestic ~12% ([UNESCO](https://www.unesco.org/reports/wwdr/en/2024/s)).

### 4.1 Efficiency measures (typically 20–40% water saving)
- **Drip irrigation** — delivers water directly to root zone, minimising evaporation and runoff, and reduces nutrient leaching ([Verdesian](https://vlsci.com/blog/water-conservation-in-agriculture))
- **Precision / smart irrigation** with soil-moisture sensors and weather-based scheduling
- **Alternate wetting and drying** for rice
- **Mulching, no-till and soil organic matter** to increase water-holding capacity
- **Line losses:** fix leaking canals and pipes before expanding supply — the cheapest "new water" in the world
- **Scheduling:** water at dawn/evening to cut evaporative loss

### 4.2 Structural measures (bigger savings)
- **Shift crop patterns** toward less water-intensive crops where appropriate ([East Asia Forum](https://eastasiaforum.org/2024/02/27/indias-thirst-for-improved-water-security))
- **Reform water and power subsidies** that incentivise over-extraction
- **Reuse treated wastewater for irrigation** — removes the largest demand from freshwater sources
- **Treat salinisation:** ~82 million hectares of rainfed cropland and 24 million hectares of irrigated land degraded by salt ([UNU](https://unu.edu/inweh/news/world-enters-era-of-global-water-bankruptcy))
- **Fix the water pricing** so scarcity is priced, while keeping basic household needs affordable
- **Drainage and waterlogging control** in irrigated command areas

### 4.3 Demand-side diet shifts
Because food dominates the water footprint, **eating lower on the food chain** in high-income countries is one of the highest-leverage individual actions available (see 1.2).

### 4.4 Irrigation in the US (a regional case)
Irrigation accounted for **47% of total US freshwater withdrawals** between 2010 and 2020 ([USDA ERS](https://www.ers.usda.gov/topics/farm-practices-management/irrigation-water-use)). Irrigated land is <17% of harvested cropland but supports >50% of total US crop sales.

## LEVEL 5 — POLICY AND GOVERNANCE (the highest-leverage level)

### 5.1 Recognize water as a human right
The UN General Assembly recognizes the right of every person to adequate, safe, accessible, affordable and equitable water. Make this **legally enforceable** at national level.

### 5.2 SDG 6 Global Acceleration Framework
A UN-wide framework launched July 2020 to put water back on track, with **five acceleration actions**: capacity development, governance, data and information, financing, and innovation ([UN DESA](https://sdgs.un.org/topics/water-and-sanitation), [UN-Water](https://www.unwater.org/our-work/sdg-6-global-acceleration-framework)).

### 5.3 The Water Action Agenda
Adopted at the 2023 UN Water Conference, built on 1,200+ stakeholder contributions. A **"gamechanger"** is defined as an initiative that addresses an entire thematic area, is sustained (not one-off), has significant potential impact, and requires **stakeholder collaboration and multiple sectors** ([UN DESA](https://sdgs.un.org/conferences/water2023/action-agenda), [ENB/IISD](https://enb.iisd.org/sdg-6-water-action-agenda-special-event)).

### 5.4 The 2026 UN Water Conference — a live opportunity
- **8–10 December 2026, Abu Dhabi, UAE**, co-hosted by the **UAE and Senegal**
- The **third global gathering of its kind since 1977**, the first co-hosted by two Global South nations, and the **first UN Conference dedicated to SDG 6**
- 478 organisations from 87 countries have contributed to date
- **Critical caveat:** the outcome will be a **summary by the President of the General Assembly rather than a negotiated outcome document**. IUCN considers clear tangible outcomes essential. ([UN-Water](https://www.unwater.org/news/2026-un-water-conference), [sdgs.un.org](https://sdgs.un.org/conferences/water2026), [IUCN](https://iucn.org/events/large-event/un-water-conference-2026), [SDG Knowledge Hub/IISD](https://sdg.iisd.org/events/2026-un-water-conference))

> **How to act:** Attend, send delegates from your organisation or government, and lobby for a negotiated outcome document rather than a chair's summary. Water governance does not get fixed by conferences alone — it needs **heads of state engagement** ([Water for Climate Pavilion](https://waterforclimate.net/news/momentum-builds-for-2026-un-water-conference-as-experts-at-cop29-demand-action)).

### 5.5 Governance priorities
- **Integrated water resources management** — water, land, and ecosystems managed as one system
- **Transboundary treaties:** only 32 of 153 reporting countries cover >90% of transboundary area with an operating agreement — expand this
- **Pollution enforcement:** regulate agricultural runoff, industrial discharge and untreated sewage
- **Water quality monitoring:** close the data gaps WMO identifies in hard-hit regions
- **Land-use zoning** to protect groundwater recharge zones and wetlands
- **National water security plans** with measurable equity targets

## LEVEL 6 — FINANCE

### 6.1 The scale of the shortfall
- Achieving universal safely managed WASH requires roughly **6× current acceleration** in drinking water, **5×** in sanitation, **3×** in hygiene ([UN Goal 6](https://sdgs.un.org/goals/goal6))
- Global progress since 2000 averages **~1% annual**; **~3% annual** is needed just to deliver basic services by 2030 ([UNSCEB](https://unsceb.org/assets/media/products/product-3678-The-SDG-6-Global-Acceleration-Framework.pdf))
- The five financing levers in the Water Action Agenda: **funding, capacity, governance, data, innovation**

### 6.2 Where the money goes, and what it returns
- Every **$1** in improved sanitation returns **~$5.5** in economic benefit

# PART C — A 12-MONTH PERSONAL / ORGANISATIONAL ACTION PLAN

| Month | Focus | Concrete deliverables |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Measure | Get an **indoor water audit** (Water Research Foundation / Water Calculator). Identify every leak. Note your council's non-revenue water rate. |
| **2** | Fix the home | Repair leaks. Fit WaterSense showerheads, faucet aerators, toilet flappers or dual-flush. Switch to full laundry loads. |
| **3** | Fix the garden | Install a smart irrigation controller and soil-moisture sensors. Mulch beds. Replace turf with native plants. |
| **4** | Fix the diet | Do a food-water-footprint audit. Plan one meat-free day a week. Cut food waste with a meal plan and proper storage. |
| **5** | Fix the plastic | Stop single-use bottles. Get a filter if tap water is unsafe. |
| **6** | Fix the catchment | Install a **rainwater harvesting** system (downpipe → filter → covered tank) for irrigation and outdoor use. |
| **7** | Map the system | Walk (or map online) your local rivers, aquifers and sewer outflows. Learn where your wastewater goes and whether it is reused. |
| **8** | Fix the policy | Write to your municipality/council about: leakage reduction, reuse mandates, and water-efficiency standards for new buildings. |
| **9** | Fix the institution | Propose a water audit, target and reduction plan at your workplace, school or place of worship. Use the **CDP Water Security** disclosure framework. |
| **10** | Fix the sector | If you farm, or influence farming: shift one field/rotation to drip irrigation or AWD, and fix canal leaks. |
| **11** | Fix the capital | Redirect money: donate, invest, or advocate for blended finance in water infrastructure. Attend a Water Action/UN Water Conference session. |
| **12** | Measure and repeat | Re-run the audit. Compare to month 1. Publish your results. Set the next year's target. |

### Metrics to track
- Gallons (or m³) used per person per day
- Gallons lost to leaks per day
- Percentage of outdoor water use eliminated
- Litres per person per day of embedded water in food
- Single-use plastic bottles avoided per year
- Litres of rainwater captured and reused
- Water/energy/carbon intensity per unit of output (for organisations)

### The three things that matter most
1. **Deploy what already works, faster** — solar pumping, rainwater harvesting, boreholes, wastewater reuse, drip irrigation, leak repair. All proven, all costed. (Sources: [Water Mission](https://watermission.org/sites/default/files/2025-01/Water%20Mission-Solar%20Pumping%20for%20Rural%20Water%20Supply.pdf), [World Bank](https://www.worldbank.org/en/topic/water/brief/solar-pumping), [GCA](https://gca.org/harvesting-rainwater-a-sustainable-solution-to-the-water-challenges-we-face), [UN-Water](https://www.unwater.org/water-facts/water-quality-and-wastewater))
2. **Fix agriculture** — it is 70% of the problem and the smallest share of public attention.
3. **Fix the money and the politics** — accelerate 6×/5×/3×, protect the 2026 UN Water Conference outcome, and treat water as a human right.

# PART E — COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS, CORRECTED

| Misconception | Reality |
|---|---|
| "It's only a problem in poor countries" | 2 billion people live in areas facing water stress **regardless of national wealth** ([WWF](https://www.worldwildlife.org/resources/facts/water-scarcity-8-facts-you-need-to-know)); 47% of US freshwater withdrawals go to irrigation |
| "Desalination will solve it" | Costs of $0.49–$2.86/m³, brine and emissions problems, energy intensity. It is **one tool**, mainly for coastal arid regions ([World Bank](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/476041552622967264/pdf/135312-WP-PUBLIC-14-3-2019-12-3-35-W.pdf)) |
| "Cutting your showers makes a difference" | True locally, but in developed economies industry is ~59% and agriculture ~29% of water use. **Household conservation is ~12%** — necessary, not sufficient |
| "It's all about drought" | Groundwater over-extraction, pollution, wetland loss and inequality are as important as rainfall |
| "2.2 billion lack water" | That is the older 2022 figure. The **current JMP 2025 figure is 2.1 billion** — improvement, but far too slow |

## Document notes

- Figures for drinking water, sanitation and hygiene are taken from the **WHO/UNICEF Joint Monitoring Programme 2025** report covering 2000–2024, published 25–26 August 2025. Where earlier figures (e.g. 2.2 billion, from the 2022 data round) appear in public materials, they are noted for clarity rather than presented as current.
- "Water scarcity," "water stress" and "lack of safely managed drinking water" are **three different measures**. Water stress is a physical supply measure; service levels are an access-and-quality measure. They are reported separately above and should not be added together.
- Percentage shares are global averages and hide enormous regional and income-level disparities. The JMP explicitly reports that LDCs are over twice as likely to lack basic WASH services.
